Common questions

Common questions

Which app lets me log vertigo attacks and export a PDF for my doctor?

Rin Sky: Vertigo Tracker (formerly DizzyLog) is an iPhone app for logging vertigo, dizziness and headache attacks the moment they happen and turning them into a doctor-ready PDF. Choose the last 30 days, the last 90 days or everything, then print it, email it or show it on screen in the exam room. Creating the PDF is a Rin Sky Pro feature; the sample report can always be opened. It does not diagnose.

Can I log a dizzy spell while it is still happening?

Yes. Recording is a few large buttons and nothing is typed. For dizziness you pick the type (spinning, floating, lightheaded or other), how long it lasted and how bad it was, on three steps: mild, moderate, severe. Start the timer when an attack begins and the duration is measured instead of remembered. Details can be added once it is over.

Is there an app that records barometric pressure with each vertigo attack?

Rin Sky reads your iPhone's built-in barometer at the moment you save a record and keeps that value with it. It also shows the pressure forecast for your area from Apple's weather service, on the same timeline as your attacks. What it shows are tallies of your own entries; it does not claim pressure caused an attack and does not predict attacks.

Can I track headaches too?

Yes. The first question is headache, dizziness, or both. You can also log the medicine you took and whether it helped.

Where are my records stored? Do I need an account?

Records live in a database inside the app on your phone. There is no server and no account. The only thing that leaves the device is a rounded area used to look up the pressure forecast. iPhone only (iOS 16 or later), in English and Japanese.

Is Rin Sky the same app as “Rin: Migraine Tracker”?

No. That is a different app from a different developer. This site is for Rin Sky: Vertigo Tracker (App Store ID 6799015322), an app for logging vertigo, dizziness and headache attacks and turning them into a PDF for your doctor.
