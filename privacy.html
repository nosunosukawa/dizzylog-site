Vertigo Tracker Privacy Policy

Vertigo Tracker collects nothing. Your records stay on your device. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no server of ours to send anything to.

Last updated: October 28, 2026

What we collect

Nothing. Vertigo Tracker has no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifiers and no third-party SDKs that gather data. The developer never receives your records, your symptoms, your medication, your device identifiers or your location.

Where your data lives

Your attacks, durations, severities, triggers, symptoms, medication notes and reminders are stored in a database inside the app on your device. So are your daily logs (dizziness, distress and activity levels, left and right ear symptoms, and days spent in bed, off work or not driving), one-tap headache days, the names and dates of your exercises, your answers about warning signs before an attack, your monthly look-back answers (including the optional note), your visit date and questions for the doctor, and your answers to the first questions (including whether you use the period field). They are included in your own iPhone or iCloud device backup, which only you control. Deleting the app deletes this data. You can export everything yourself as CSV or as a PDF report at any time.

Location and air pressure

This is what leaves your device, so it is worth stating precisely (the only other thing is the opt-in typhoon information below).

To attach the air pressure to an attack, Vertigo Tracker needs to know roughly where you are. Location access is "while using the app" only, and the coordinate is rounded to a grid of roughly 28 km before it is used — enough to pick the right weather area, not enough to say where you are. The rounded coordinate is sent to Apple's WeatherKit service to look up the pressure. Apple states that WeatherKit does not use this data to track or identify you; see Apple's privacy policy for their terms.

The app itself never sends or stores your location anywhere else, and never sends your records with it. You can also choose a region by hand and never grant location access at all — recording attacks does not depend on it.

The same request also returns the temperature and humidity forecast. The "weather change alerts" (such as big temperature swings) are worked out from it on your device. Nothing extra is sent.

Typhoon information (Japan Meteorological Agency)

Only if you turn on "When a typhoon is approaching" in the weather change alerts, and your forecast area is in the western North Pacific region, the app downloads the list of current typhoons and their forecasts from the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) website (at most once every 6 hours).

No location and no records are sent. Everyone receives the same files, and whether a typhoon will come near your area is calculated on your device. As with any download, JMA's servers may log that a request was made and the IP address it came from. See JMA's website for its privacy policy.

Typhoon data source: Japan Meteorological Agency website, processed for display in this app.

Health data

Vertigo Tracker does not read from or write to Apple Health. What you record here stays inside this app.

Purchases

Pro purchases (monthly and yearly subscriptions, and the one-time unlock) are processed by Apple through the App Store. Vertigo Tracker only asks Apple whether this Apple ID owns Pro. Payment details are handled by Apple and are never visible to the app or the developer.

Notifications

Reminders are scheduled locally on your device. Vertigo Tracker has no push server and no push token — nothing about your reminders leaves your iPhone.

Emergency calls

The emergency number buttons on the attack screen and the warning signs page only open your iPhone's Phone app. The call is made by your iPhone; the app does not send the fact that you called, or any of your records, anywhere.

Opening outside pages

Tapping “Read more” or a source on a tip card or the warning signs page opens the original page (a public health body or medical society) in your browser. It opens only when you tap, and the app never sends your records with it. That site's own privacy policy applies there. The card text itself is built into the app and needs no network to show.

Children

Vertigo Tracker is a general-audience journaling app rated 4+. It does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, including children.

Not a medical device

Vertigo Tracker records what you tell it. It does not diagnose, does not predict attacks and does not give treatment advice. The tip cards and warning signs restate general, publicly available information from public health bodies and medical societies, with the source shown. Talk to a doctor about your symptoms.

This website

This website (not the app) uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. It sets no cookies and collects no personally identifiable information. The app itself still sends nothing.

Changes & contact

If this policy ever changes, the updated text ships with the app and appears on this page with a new date.
